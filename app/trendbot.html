How the rule works, and what to watch out for
  • The rule. TrendBot compares each day's closing price to the average of the last 200 closes. It says IN once the price closes more than 1% above that average and OUT once it closes more than 1% below. In between, it keeps its last answer, so small wiggles don't make it flip.
  • It's always a little late. It sells after a drop has started and buys after a recovery has started. It's built to get you out of long, deep slides like 2008, not to catch tops and bottoms.
  • Sideways markets cost money. When prices bounce around the average, it can sell low and buy back higher a few times in a row.
  • It often makes less than holding during long rising markets. What it usually gives you is a smaller worst-case drop.
  • Taxes. In a regular brokerage account each sale can be taxable. In an IRA or 401(k) it isn't.
  • The back-test acts on each signal at the next day's close, charges 0.05% per switch, and assumes cash earns the yield you set. Past results don't guarantee future ones. This is a tool, not financial advice.
